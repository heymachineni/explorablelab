# Role-as-System

> **One-line essence:** Play the antagonist, not the hero — inhabit the force that drives the system to feel why it persists.

## What it is

Role-as-system assigns the learner a **structural role** inside the phenomenon — the anxiety voice, the engagement algorithm, the news camera, the warden, the defecting strategy — rather than the sympathetic protagonist trying to escape it. Controls map to the system's incentives, not the victim's coping tools.

Insight comes from **inside the loop**: you press the button that maximizes outrage because that is your job in the simulation. Pamphlets describe systemic forces; roles make them felt.

## When to use it

Use role-as-system when the target misunderstanding is **agency misattribution** — blaming individuals for outcomes produced by roles, incentives, or feedback.

High-value contexts:

- Mental health (anxiety as protective subroutine, not enemy)
- Media economics (clicks drive coverage)
- Institutional harm (prison guard, obedient experimenter)
- Game-theoretic defection (you are the short-term optimizer)

Avoid when the ethical frame requires centering lived experience of harm without role-play, or when the role would trivialize trauma without careful design and content warnings.

## How it works in an explorable

1. **Cast the user** — explicit: "You are the anxiety. Your job is to keep them safe."
2. **Map controls to systemic incentives** — not "calm down" but "raise alert level"
3. **Show downstream effects on a separate character or population** — the human pays the cost
4. **Remove escape hatches early** — if the user can simply "choose kindness," the role collapses
5. **Offer reframe** — compassion for the role, not just blame; systems persist because they once worked

The learner should finish thinking "of course that button exists" — then notice who built the dashboard.

## Design notes

- **First person for the role, third person for the harmed** — clarity of perspective
- **Short sessions** — inhabiting antagonist roles is emotionally taxing; brevity respects the user
- **Content warnings and skip** — mandatory for obedience, harassment, or carceral frames
- **Avoid cartoon villainy** — the role should feel reasonable moment to moment
- **Pair with [[but-chain]]** — "Yes, anxiety protects you → but never shutting off → therefore avoidance spirals"
- **Classroom facilitation** — debrief is part of the design, not an afterthought

## Anti-patterns

- **Hero fantasy** — user always saves the day; role-play becomes power trip
- **Straw antagonist** — mustache-twirling evil nobody identifies with
- **No consequences panel** — playing the algorithm without seeing human impact is edutainment noise
- **Real identity data** — do not use learners' personal information as simulation fuel
- **Role without agency** — on rails "pretend you're bad" with one obvious correct choice
